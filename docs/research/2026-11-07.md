# Research note — 2026-11-07

Second entry in the course's research log. Rule for this log: every note records what was
read, what it implies for **this** course, what was decided, and what is still unknown.

This note is the audit of the five sources the teaching design rests on. The register in
`docs/reading.md` now carries the evidence itself — page range and a quoted line per source —
so this note records what the reading *did to the argument*, which the register cannot.

## What this implies for `rust_lessons`

1. **The Subgoals blocks are half a finding.** Section prose carries a **Subgoals** block in
   §07, §08, §11 and §16 and cites the subgoal-label result. That result, in the paper that
   established it for *text*, was conditional: a labelled text alone did not improve
   performance. The course therefore needs the labelled **example** beside the block (a worked
   example whose steps carry the same subgoal names), or the block should stop citing this
   finding. This is the one place where reading the source changed a teaching claim rather than a
   footnote. **Resolved in 0.20.0:** each of the four sections now carries a `# Worked example`
   whose steps repeat its subgoal labels word for word and end in an `Output:` line that was run,
   and `tests/curriculum.rs::subgoals_travel_with_a_worked_example` fails if either half goes
   missing. What the code cannot show is whether the pairing helps a *Rust* learner — that is the
   measurement this course still does not have.
2. **The Parsons row may claim less than it did.** The 2022 review defines the problem type and
   surveys the claims; the course can say Parsons problems are a scaffolded alternative to
   writing from scratch and are widely studied. It cannot say they work without replicating a
   study, and the review says the field has not.
3. **Faded Parsons are not supported by perception data.** Nine participants who chose their own
   scaffold is precisely the design that cannot show an effect; it is good evidence that the
   *variant* matters and worth keeping for that reason.
4. **Expertise reversal is a real effect and the course already handles it the right way** by
   saying who the book is not for. Nothing to change; the citation is now honest about being
   abstract-deep.

## Decisions taken from this pass

* A `read` in `docs/reading.md` carries an evidence tag, and the block it points at states the
  pages looked at and quotes the source. `tests/curriculum.rs::every_read_source_has_a_quoted_line`
  enforces it bi-directionally: a `read` row without a tag fails, a `read` block without a quote
  fails, and a register that is entirely (or not at all) `read` fails, because a status column
  that cannot discriminate is decoration.
* The by-section rows keep their depth words. Most sources there were used at title level and
  saying so is the honest state; the audit was about the five claims the design rests on.
* Corrections are recorded in the register with their evidence (three of them now), rather than
  silently rewritten — a wrong citation that leaves no trace teaches nobody.

## Still unknown / next searches

* Joentausta & Hellas's **result** — the abstract states the design, not the outcome. Needs
  institutional access; until then the register says `skimmed`.
* Whether subgoal labels help when the example is *Rust* rather than App Inventor blocks. The
  published evidence is drag-and-drop blocks and written problem-solving steps; Rust's compiler
  messages may already supply what the labels supply.
* Whether a labelled example beside a **Subgoals** block is achievable in generated
  `problems/section_NN_faded.md` (the faded Parsons artefacts are the natural carrier: they
  already have the steps and the blanks). **Partly answered by building it:** the example went
  into the lesson prose instead, because `parsons` takes its lines from the module's functions
  (`pick_function`), not from prose, so the artefacts carry code and no subgoal names. A faded
  problem that repeated the labels would need the generator to emit them from the `# Subgoals`
  block — a change nobody has costed, and one that would put two copies of the same labels in
  the learner's path.
